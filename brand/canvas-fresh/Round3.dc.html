Five settings you can point at

Round 2 stayed with abstract ideas (merge, override). This round uses things people actually flip, nest or tick when they configure: switches, toggles, indented keys, key = value, checked rows. Chunkier and rounder, no gear. Same ink plus one orange-red.

1 DIP switch

Says configuration: a bank of DIP switches, the hardware way of setting a machine up before software had config files. Knobs step down in a diagonal, a Z in motion.

Risk: reads as a retro or hardware icon, and three slots can look like a volume mixer.

2 Toggles

Says configuration: three on/off settings switches stacked, the shape of every settings screen. Knobs go right, left, right: a Z drawn by the switch positions.

Risk: the most UI-icon of the five; the Z is only implied.

3 Nested

Says configuration: indented key rows, the way HOCON, YAML and TOML nest settings. The coloured row is the value you asked for, deep in the tree.

Risk: reads as a plain list or a chart; no obvious ZIO link.

4 Equals

Says configuration: key = value: the sign every setting is written with, offset so it steps like a Z. Top bar is the key, coloured bar is the value.

Risk: very simple, so it can look like a menu or a placeholder; the Z is faint.

5 Validated

Says configuration: rows of settings with a check box each. Two values accepted, the coloured one rejected: ZIO Config reports errors, it does not guess.

Risk: busy at small sizes, and a red cross can read as an error badge for the brand.
